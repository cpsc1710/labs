02 / Change the system

What would you change?

You have more options than turning the knob. Which changes reach deepest into the system?

These twelve cards paraphrase Rising’s thermostat discussion. The knob supplies level 12; his blog then illustrates levels 11 through 1.

1. Rank the interventions

Use ↑ and ↓ to arrange the cards from the least to the most leverage. Rank the kind of change, rather than its convenience or cost. Discuss your order before opening the matching stage.

2. Name the change

Match each card to a level. Each level should be used once. You can still revise your ranking. Meadows’s numbering runs from 12 (least) to 1 (most).

Compare your interpretation with Rising’s. His examples are discussion prompts; some do not demonstrate the named mechanism on their own.

Top: least leverageBottom: most leverage

Where does the analogy break?

Faster recovery alone does not establish a reinforcing loop (#7). Ask what change feeds back to amplify itself. Likewise, leaving a job or closing a website can change a person’s participation without changing the warehouse’s rules or anyone’s paradigm. Can you make those examples more precise?

Meadows’s ordering describes possible leverage, not guaranteed results. Explain what would have to be true for an intervention to work.
